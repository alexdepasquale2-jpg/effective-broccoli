Anatomy of a wrapped book

The tag is the whole sales pitch

Nobody picks up a brown paper brick. They pick up four lines of handwriting that describe a feeling they already want. Write these with a fine liner, in your own hand, every time.

Spice  ·  CW: violence, grief, on-page death

  1. Four tropes, no plotNever summarize the story — you'd spoil the date. Name the flavors: found family, one bed, touch her and die, rivals with a deadline.
  2. Spice level, alwaysFive dots, filled to taste. It's the single most-asked question at a romance table and it saves you having the conversation forty times.
  3. Content warnings, alwaysNon-negotiable on dark romance. Someone unwrapping something they weren't warned about is how you get a bad day, not a brand.
  4. Page count and ending"HEA promised" or "bring tissues." People choose an ending the way they choose a movie on a Friday night.
  5. Seal it and leave it shutWax seal, twine, no peeking window. The unwrap is the thing you're actually selling.

Before the first paid market

Massachusetts, in two errands

  • File a DBA at the Wilmington town clerk — "doing business as Foley and Fable." $25–$65, good for four years. This is what lets you take payment under the name and open a business account later.
  • Register on MassTaxConnect — free. Craft goods are taxable at 6.25%. Collect it, don't eat it: either build it into $16 / $28 or add it at checkout, but pick one and stay consistent.
  • Skip the LLC. Sole prop plus DBA is enough until there's money you'd be upset to lose.
  • Skip the website, skip the terminal upgrade. Square's free card reader and Instagram DMs cover the first twenty sales.
  • Resale is legal — used books are yours to sell. No library discards, and nothing you wouldn't defend to the person who unwraps it.
